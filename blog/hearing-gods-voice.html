Hearing God’s Voice

Developing the consciousness of a child of God

About 15 minutes to read · with things to try

One word from God can change a life completely. A single sentence from Him can redirect a journey, open a new door, bring clarity in confusion and turn an ordinary life into a purposeful one.

When God speaks, His word carries His power, His direction and His provision.

01Everything is in the voice of God

God’s voice carries more than information. His word holds direction and purpose. When God wants to provide for us, He does not always place the provision straight into our hands. Often He first speaks a word that gives direction, and as we respond to that word, the provision follows.

God’s power is joined to His voice. When He speaks, His voice carries His power, and that voice draws provision towards us. What we need, even our finances, lies behind His word. Everything we need for our life and our purpose is held within the voice of God.

Do not only seek what God can give you. Seek to hear what God is saying.

When we hear His voice, we understand His direction, His purpose and what He has prepared for us. Everything God has purposed for our lives is connected to His word, and His voice holds the direction for our destiny.

02The qualification: a child of God

The first qualification for hearing God’s voice is that you are a child of God, and that you know it. Carry that awareness twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week, not only while you pray or sit in church. Keep this knowing alive inside you: I am a child of God.

If we lose that consciousness, His voice becomes hard to hear clearly.

My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.John 10:27

A sheep hears its shepherd because it knows him. There is a relationship between them. In the same way, when we know we belong to God, we become more sensitive to His voice.

Not a beggar, not a worker, but a child

Many of us come to God like beggars, bringing only our needs and asking for one thing after another. Others come like workers before an owner. Our relationship with God is neither. It is the relationship of a Father and His child.

How do you come to God?

The beggar

How they come
Only with needs, asking for one thing after another.
What fills their mind
What they lack, and what they must ask for next.
What they miss
The position and the inheritance that already belong to a child.

The worker

How they come
To receive instructions and carry out duties. An owner does not need to speak personally with every worker.
What fills their mind
What they have done, what they have failed to do, and what they still have to earn.
What they miss
The voice of a Father, because they relate to God as an employer.

The child

How they come
With confidence, knowing they belong to Him.
What fills their mind
Who their Father is, and whose they are.
What follows
They relate to God from sonship, so His voice becomes easier to hear.

When we lose the consciousness of being His child, the devil finds a way into our thinking. The enemy wants us to forget who we are and to doubt whether we really belong to God. So hold on to it: I am a child of God.

Even as a child, I may fail in many ways. I may make mistakes, face hard situations and go through failures, but I never lose my standing as His child. My circumstances may change. My identity does not.

Beyond what He does, to who He is

We often see God as Healer, Provider or Warrior. He is all of these, but we must look beyond what He does for us and see Him as our Father. Healing, provision and protection all flow out of that relationship.

When we relate to Him as His children, from the position of sonship, hearing His voice becomes natural.

03The consciousness of the Father

In prayer we usually call Him God or Lord, and there is nothing wrong with that. But often we do not carry the awareness of a child speaking to a Father. The issue is not the word we use. It is whether we come with a child’s consciousness.

Ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father.Romans 8:15

We have received the Spirit of adoption, the Spirit of sonship, and by Him we cry “Abba, Father.” The Holy Spirit makes us aware that God is our Father and settles in us the confidence that we belong to Him.

What is the Spirit of sonship? It is the DNA of Jesus Christ in us. Through Christ we receive the identity and the consciousness of God’s children, so we can say with boldness, “I am His child.” This is not meant to come and go with our circumstances, successes, failures or feelings. Carry it every day, in every situation.

When this consciousness grows in you, things change:

  • Your approach to God changes.
  • Your prayer changes.
  • Your confidence changes.
  • Your relationship with Him changes.
  • And His voice becomes clearer to you.

So develop the consciousness of a child of God. Do not come to Him as a beggar, nor with the mind of a worker. Come as His child, know Him as your Father, and let the Spirit of sonship build boldness in you, until deep in your heart you can always say it.

Make it your own

I am a child of God.He is my Father.I belong to Him,and I desire to hear His voice.

04Trust God, not prayer

Be careful not to put your trust in prayer instead of in God. Prayer must never become the object of our faith. When we trust prayer more than the One we pray to, we can make it an idol without realising. Prayer has no power of its own. God is the power behind prayer. Prayer is a relationship with Him, and its power comes from that relationship.

No one stands in between

When we pray, we can be confident that God Himself hears and answers. There is no one standing in the middle: we pray, and Jesus answers. We do not need another person to recommend us before God so that He will bless us. That thought is wrong. No recommendation is needed. What we need is a good, direct relationship with Him.

Blessings may reach us through different people, opportunities or situations. God may use someone to help us, but our trust must not shift to that person or that method. It stays with God: He will hear my prayer. He will act for me. Prayer is a personal relationship between God and us.

A love that will not share His place

God is possessive of His children. He will not give up the right and authority He has over our lives, and He will not allow anyone or anything to take His place. No one else can stand where He stands, and no one can love us the way He does. His love cannot be compared with any person’s love. It stands in a place of its own.

He stays in the same place in our lives. When we change, He does not. When relationships fail, He remains faithful. He will not abandon us, deceive us or let us down. So keep your trust wholly in Him, not in prayer, people or circumstances.

Prayer is two-way

Prayer is not only speaking. It is not talking continuously before God and then walking away. Prayer is communication, and communication needs both speaking and listening.

What does your prayer look like?

One-way: we talk, then leave

We pour out our hearts, then we become quiet and give Him our attention. Do not be in such a hurry to finish that you never stop to listen. If prayer is truly a relationship with God, it has to flow in both directions.

1:00

Try it now

One quiet minute

Tell God briefly what is on your heart. Then stay quiet for one minute and give Him your attention.

So do not put your trust in prayer itself. Put it in God. Do not make prayer an idol; make it a relationship. Go to Him directly, with confidence and boldness, knowing that He hears you, He loves you and He will never abandon you.

Prayer is two-way communication between God and His child.

05Pleasing God and people

And the child Samuel grew on, and was in favour both with the LORD, and also with men.1 Samuel 2:26

Samuel grew in a way that pleased both God and people. In ministry our task is not only to please God. We must also learn to walk rightly with people, so that our life and ministry carry a good testimony before both.

God cares about the ministry, and He also cares about the person doing it.

And the child Samuel ministered unto the LORD before Eli.1 Samuel 3:1

Samuel served the Lord, but he served before Eli. Ministry involves relationship, responsibility, submission and accountability to spiritual leaders.

When God places us under a leader, we honour that position and stay accountable. Leaders must not misuse their authority or take away the rights of those who serve with them. And we should willingly give our leaders the place, honour and authority God has given them. Submission is not losing your identity or being controlled by someone. It is learning to work within the order God has set.

If you refuse to submit to any leader, it is hard to become everything God wants you to be. A person who cannot receive correction, guidance, instruction or accountability will limit their own growth. We need people who can speak into our lives, correct us, guide us and help us develop. Samuel learned to serve the Lord while accountable to Eli.

Serve God sincerely before your leaders, stay accountable, and let God shape you into who He wants you to be.

06Learning through correction

In Matthew 17 a father brings his son to Jesus. The disciples had tried and failed to cast out the demon. Jesus corrected them openly, disappointed that they had not used what they had already been taught.

What matters is what happened next. The disciples did not take offence or rebel. No one asked, “Why did You scold us in front of everyone?” They did not make it about their reputation or question His authority. They came to Him with one question:

Then came the disciples to Jesus apart, and said, Why could not we cast him out?Matthew 17:19

Their focus was not on defending themselves but on understanding where they had failed and how to do better. A true disciple has a learning attitude.

When you are corrected

  • “Why did you correct me?”
  • “Why in front of everyone?”
  • “What will people think of me?”
  • “What can I learn from this?”
  • “Where did I go wrong?”
  • “What should I change?”

This heart stops growing. Switch to see the other.

The relationship between Jesus and His disciples gives us a clear pattern. A disciple follows, learns, receives instruction, accepts correction and puts what was learned into practice. Leadership is more than giving instructions, and discipleship is more than listening. Both are a process of learning and obeying.

The disciples did not reach spiritual heights just by being near Jesus. They grew through what they learned and obeyed: watching Him, listening to Him, receiving His correction and following His instructions, until their understanding and authority matured.

So if we want to grow, we need two qualities:

A willingness to learnA willingness to obey

Do not be offended when you are corrected, and do not focus on how the correction was given while missing what God wants to teach you. Jesus did not only call disciples to follow Him; He called them to learn from Him and become like Him. The path to maturity is not just being associated with a leader. It is accountability and responsibility.

07Samuel: serving before knowing

Samuel pleased the Lord. He was already ministering, and his life was given to God’s service. Yet Scripture says something striking about him:

Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD, neither was the word of the LORD yet revealed unto him.1 Samuel 3:7

Samuel was serving, yet he did not yet know the Lord in this way, and the word of the Lord had not yet been revealed to him. His ministry was real, but his own experience of hearing God had not yet begun. We can know about God, serve Him and even be busy in ministry, and still need to learn to recognise His voice.

Samuel’s journey, and ours. Tap each step.

Serving the Lord

Samuel ministered to the Lord before Eli. Faithful service is good, but it is not yet the whole relationship God wants.

Knowing the Lord

Coming to know the Lord Himself, not only knowing about Him. The aim is not to collect information about God but to come into a relationship with Him.

Hearing His voice

Recognising the voice of the Lord. This was Samuel’s turning point. From that moment his relationship with God entered a new dimension, and his life and ministry were never the same again.

Knowing God and hearing God belong together. As our relationship with Him grows, so must our ability to recognise His voice. God wanted more for Samuel than service. He wanted Samuel to hear Him.

08The night God called Samuel

The encounter between Samuel and Eli shows that there is a position from which we hear the voice of God. Walk through the night, one scene at a time.

  1. Scene 1 of 5

    The lamp is still burning

    And ere the lamp of God went out in the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was, and Samuel was laid down to sleep.1 Samuel 3:3

    It is night. Samuel is lying in his place in the temple, near the lamp of God.

  2. Scene 2 of 5

    A voice in the night

    The LORD called Samuel: and he answered, Here am I. And he ran unto Eli, and said, Here am I; for thou calledst me. And he said, I called not; lie down again.1 Samuel 3:4–5

    Samuel hears the voice, but he does not yet know whose it is. He thinks it is Eli.

  3. Scene 3 of 5

    Again, and again

    And the LORD called Samuel again the third time. And he arose and went to Eli… And Eli perceived that the LORD had called the child.1 Samuel 3:8

    Samuel had the voice but not the experience to recognise it. Eli had the experience, though the voice was not coming to him in the same way. Because of his experience, Eli understood what was happening and could guide Samuel.

  4. Scene 4 of 5

    Go back to your place

    Therefore Eli said unto Samuel, Go, lie down: and it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, Speak, LORD; for thy servant heareth. So Samuel went and lay down in his place.1 Samuel 3:9

    Samuel could not stay wherever he liked and expect to understand the voice. He had to return to the position where God was speaking to him.

  5. Scene 5 of 5

    Speak, for your servant hears

    And the LORD came, and stood, and called as at other times, Samuel, Samuel. Then Samuel answered, Speak; for thy servant heareth.1 Samuel 3:10

    Back in his place, Samuel recognises the voice. From this moment his life and ministry are never the same.

The voice of God and the voice of the leader

While God was calling, Samuel took the voice for Eli’s. Sometimes God’s voice and the voice of our leader can sound alike, because God may speak to us through the instruction, guidance or correction of the leader He has placed over us. So do not be quick to reject the voice of your leader.

Stay in position

Samuel heard God’s voice when he was in position. When we step out of position, we can lose our sensitivity to Him. So we must create a place to hear the voice of God.

A place

Somewhere in your home that is set apart for meeting God.

An atmosphere

Quiet and attention, so His voice is not crowded out.

A position

A place you return to regularly, as Samuel lay down in his place.

09From outside to inside

God’s nature never changes, but the covenant through which He relates to His people has. The New Covenant came through Jesus Christ and was sealed by His death and resurrection.

Where does the light come from?

God was with His people, and they experienced His voice mainly from outside. He spoke to Moses, Samuel and Elijah, often calling them from without.

If any man walk in the day, he stumbleth not, because he seeth the light of this world. But if a man walk in the night, he stumbleth, because there is no light in him.John 11:9–10

Jesus spoke of walking in the light, and of stumbling when there is no light within. Under the Old Covenant, God’s light and direction were experienced from outside. Under the New Covenant, His light is meant to work inside every believer.

The change from the Old Covenant to the New is a movement from the outside to the inside: from external revelation and direction to the presence, guidance and life of God within us through the Holy Spirit.

10John was the voice, Jesus is the Word

John the Baptist called himself the voice of one crying in the wilderness. He was sent to prepare the way for the Lord: to proclaim the message and make people’s hearts ready for Jesus. Jesus was not just a voice carrying a message. He is the Word Himself. Tap each card.

John was the voice that announced the Word; Jesus is the living Word. A voice communicates a message, but the Word is the message itself. John became the instrument through which God’s message was proclaimed, and Jesus was its fulfilment.

In the same way, our lives can become instruments through which God’s Word is expressed. Let His Word fill your heart, guide your thoughts and shape your actions. As the Holy Spirit leads you, your words and your life can carry His message to others.
