A study in leadership

Spiritual Leadership

Serving, teaching, listening, and raising others to lead

01

The foundation: learn, teach, serve

Never stop learning, and never stop teaching. A spiritual leader keeps his focus on people and works at becoming someone who can teach them well.

Two things matter most: a good relationship with God and a good relationship with people. The first should show in the second. How a leader treats people reveals how close he really is to God.

Spiritual leadership guides and serves. It does not control.

Worldly leadership often rests on control and domination, as the example of Hitler shows. Spiritual leadership takes the opposite road: it walks with people and lifts them up instead of ruling over them.

02

Vision and character

Every leader has to answer two questions: Where am I going? and Where am I taking the people I lead?

A leader must also grow in character: in love, care, faithfulness and righteousness. Gifts may open doors, but character keeps a leader standing.

Vision gives you clarity about where you are and where you are headed. When you wake up each morning you know what you are there to do. A person without vision is blind in a real sense, even if his eyes work perfectly.

03

Raised to meet a need

Servant leadership and compassion

God heard the cry of the Israelites and raised up Moses to lead them out. That tells us something: people do not exist to serve the leader. The leader is called to serve the people.

Matthew 9:35–36 describes crowds that were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd. A good leader takes the time to know and understand the people around him. Jesus then said the harvest is plentiful but the workers are few (Matthew 9:37).

Raised to meet a need (Matthew 10:1)

Jesus sent out His twelve disciples. God raises and sends leaders because He sees what people are going through. A leader's vision is not there to make him comfortable. It is there to bring answers to people's problems.

You are the solution

You, and the anointing you carry, are meant to be part of the answer to people's problems. Jesus is the ultimate medicine, and you carry that medicine to others. Your mission is to rescue people from the authority of the devil.

04

Calling, accountability and vision

Your calling has the power to push you forward, but you are accountable for it, and accountable for what you learn. Make this your declaration: “I am not independent; I am accountable.”

Do not hand unreasonable excuses to God or to your leader.

Two kinds of leaders

Leadership begins in two ways. In the first, God calls a leader directly. In the second, leaders call and appoint other leaders. The Bible shows both: the Father called Jesus, and Jesus appointed the twelve.

How vision connects leaders and people

When God gives a vision, the leader recognises it, and then the people find both the leader and the vision. In order: vision creates a leader, people find that leader, and people embrace the vision. Every personal vision is tied to the leader's vision, yet each person still has to discover his own path.

Discover your path and your gifts

Work out your own path and find your spiritual gifts. They may lie in media, evangelism, administration, prophecy, healing, or casting out demons.

05

Teaching at the people's level

You have to build your ability to teach on purpose. Teaching does not always come naturally. Some abilities are inborn; others must be developed and strengthened over time.

In the old story, a thirsty crow cannot reach the water in a narrow-necked pot, so it drops pebbles in one by one until the water rises. A good teacher does the same. He adapts to the situation and comes down to the people's level, so that he can teach them effectively and meet their actual needs.

Teaching and learning go hand in hand. If you stop adapting and stop learning, you will not be able to teach anyone else.

06

Jesus: the model and the bridge

Jesus had full divinity, yet He humbled Himself and came in human form to reach us. He came to understand humanity from the inside, and He knew the true nature of human beings.

Why He is the only mediator

If Jesus were only divine, there would be no true mediator between God and people. As God, He fully understands divinity. As a man, He fully understands humanity, in both its good and its broken sides. That is why He is the only mediator of the New Covenant.

Because He knows divinity, He brings God's message to us. Because He has felt human weakness, He carries our needs and struggles to God.

He knew our struggle

Jesus personally knew hunger, thirst, pain and grief, including deep hurt and betrayal. He understands human pain because He came down to where we are.

Meet people where they are. Do not wait for them to reach your level first.

A leader must do the same: step down to the level of the people he leads, so that he understands them truthfully.

07

Leading without control

Spiritual leadership is not dictatorship and does not mean running every detail. A true spiritual leader does not dominate, manipulate or intimidate. A leader is not a manager either. A manager keeps things as they are; a leader moves people forward.

Teach, do not control

Ministry means keeping the Word of God and teaching people from it. People should be taught, not controlled. Domination, manipulation and intimidation are the tactics of witchcraft, not the nature of God.

God honours free will

Respect for free will is part of God's own nature. He did not call a committee before creating heaven and earth; He made them out of His own desire and joy. Yet He gives people real choices and does not force obedience. In Eden He set the Tree of Life and the Tree of Death before humanity and let them choose. God takes no delight in anything done by force.

Hate sin, not just fear it

Do not teach people on the footing that God will punish them. That produces only a passing fear of sin. The goal is not to fear sin but to hate it, and that comes from relationship. As children of God we share His nature, so what He dislikes should become what we dislike. Joseph said it this way: how could he do such a wicked thing and sin against God (Genesis 39:9)? We turn from sin out of love for God, not merely out of fear.

Lead by example

A leader should be a living example and never force anyone. Do not force people to pray; let them choose to pray. Do not force people to read the Bible; let them choose to read it. God loves those who choose freely to love Him, not those who act under pressure. Loving God, praying, serving and reading the Word should all flow from free will.

08

The leader as counsellor

A leader must become a good counsellor and work on those skills on purpose. Someone who talks endlessly is not a good counsellor. A good counsellor is first of all a good listener.

You have to understand the condition of the people you lead, and you only learn their problems by listening carefully. Ask yourself: am I just a talker, or am I a listener? The more deeply you listen, the wiser you become. The Spirit of Wisdom rests on those who listen.

A good leader is also a good counsellor, so ministry should not depend only on a stage and a microphone. Learn to counsel people one to one and give them your full attention, including steady eye contact while you listen.

Intentional, like Jesus

Jesus did not meet the Samaritan woman by accident. He went there on purpose, knowing she would come, and chose to speak to someone society treated as a nobody. He also held a deep conversation with Nicodemus late at night, and that conversation played a real part in Nicodemus's journey toward Christ.

The main aim of leaders and counsellors is never to hurt or wound people, but to care for them on purpose and lead them to Jesus.

The wise counsellor and personal teacher

Jesus is the finest example of a wise counsellor. A good teacher does not ignore a weak or struggling student. He brings the student close, sits down with him, and helps him through his struggles. Jesus did not spend all His time with the crowds. He made room for individuals: the Samaritan woman, Nicodemus, and His own disciples.

09

Raising leaders and finishing well

The mark of a successful leader

A truly successful leader raises other successful leaders. Before your life ends, train and develop the next generation. Even if only ten people are in your care, invest in them and raise at least one leader from among them. Do not let believers stay followers forever without ever moving into leadership.

Believer→Disciple→Leader

When someone becomes a leader, that person carries the process on by raising new leaders. This steady multiplication is how a successful leader works.

Finishing well

The end of a person's life and ministry matters more than the beginning. As Ecclesiastes 7:8 says, the end of a matter is better than its beginning. A recommended book on this is God's Generals: The Untold Story.

  • 1 Timothy 6:12 Fight the good fight of faith (compare 2 Timothy 4:7).
  • 2 Timothy 4:7 Finish the race and keep the faith.
  • 1 Corinthians 11:1 “Follow my example, as I follow the example of Christ.”

Inner character and spiritual growth

A leader must be trustworthy, reliable and faithful with what God entrusts to him. Give deliberate attention to your inner spiritual life. Real success in ministry is measured by how much you grow in your spirit.

The kingly anointing in the marketplace

Daniel, Esther, Joseph and David carried a kingly anointing. They served God faithfully in government and in public life while staying devoted to Him. This is marketplace ministry. God also anoints believers for priestly ministry and spiritual service.

The church should not only train priests to serve inside its walls. It should also raise up kings who carry God's anointing into society and leadership.
